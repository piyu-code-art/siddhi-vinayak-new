Quality, testing and export documentation

Quality in agricultural exports is not a slogan, it is a set of numbers and a set of certificates. This page explains how we sample a lot, what we test, which documents are issued for a consignment, and where we draw the line on claims.

Sampling and testing

What we test, and on what basis

A lot is defined in the offer: the same origin, the same processing run and the same packing specification. Sampling is done from the packed lot, not from a showpiece sample, and analyses are carried out by NABL-accredited laboratories.

Parameters commonly agreed for each product range
Range Parameters normally agreed for the lot
Spices Moisture, purity and foreign matter, total and acid-insoluble ash, the value parameter for the spice (ASTA colour, curcumin or volatile oil), aflatoxin B1 and total aflatoxin, heavy metals, pesticide residues against the destination maximum residue limits, the ethylene oxide screen where the destination requires it, and dye screens where colour is the selling point.
Rice Moisture, broken grains, foreign matter, average grain length, chalky and damaged grains, and the pesticide residue panel for the destination market.
Makhana Moisture, breakage, kernel size distribution, heavy metals, aflatoxin and, for ready-to-eat packed product, a microbiology panel. Purity checks cover the raw kernel, not the finished pack.
Fresh produce Grade by size or count, colour and firmness, expected transit life at the agreed temperature, and the pesticide residue panel the destination market applies.

Who tests, and how

  • Samples are drawn from the packed lot by someone other than the person who packed it.
  • Samples are sealed, labelled with the lot code, and sent to an NABL-accredited laboratory.
  • Counter-sample retention, re-test rights and the cost of any third-party inspection are agreed in the contract rather than assumed.
  • If a result sits close to the destination limit, we tell you before shipment instead of shipping and hoping.

What a test report is, and is not

A laboratory report describes the samples it received on the day it received them. It is not a guarantee that every unit in a container will test identically. That is why we agree a sampling plan, why the specification sheet is the contractual document, and why a pre-shipment sample is offered on every first order.

Documentation

The document set for a consignment

Use this as a planning list, not as a legal schedule. Which documents are mandatory, who issues them and what wording they must carry differs by product and destination, and the position changes from time to time. We confirm the exact set for your shipment with the clearing agent and, where relevant, against the importing country's requirements before the goods move.

Packing, marking and storage

How the goods are packed and marked

Bulk and retail

Lined PP woven or jute bags in 10, 25 and 50 kg, or kraft and laminated packs for retail. A food-grade liner is specified for every milled and ready-to-eat product.

Makhana packs

Bulk 10 and 20 kg double-lined bags, or retail packs under your own brand subject to artwork approval and the declarations your market requires on the pack.

Fresh produce packs

Mesh or jute bags for onion and coconut, ventilated and lined cartons for chilli, banana and pomegranate, palletised where the lane allows it, with wood treated to ISPM 15.

Marking

Buyer marks, product and variety or grade, lot code, net and gross weight, country of origin, and any destination-specific declaration agreed in the contract.

Storage before dispatch

Dry, ventilated, pest-monitored stores away from sunlight and odorous goods. Fresh produce moves through a cold room rather than a warehouse.

Load plan

Container stuffing is done under supervision with photographs, and container cleanliness is checked before loading. Reefers are pre-tripped and the set point recorded.

Shelf life and temperature

For dry goods, trade guidance is 12 months in unopened, sealed packing held below about 25 °C and away from direct light; ground spices lose their top notes faster than the whole form. Makhana needs to stay dry and airtight because it is hygroscopic.

For fresh produce, shelf life is a function of temperature and time rather than a fixed number. We state the expected transit life for the lot against the agreed set point, and we would rather decline a lane we cannot hold the temperature on than send fruit that arrives soft.

Traceability

Every pack carries a lot code. That code links back to the source and the arrival lot, the processing date and line, the packing record, the laboratory report and the invoice. If a buyer raises an issue, we can pull the whole history of that lot and identify where else it went.

  • One lot code per processing run; no mixing after testing.
  • Consignment files kept for at least three years, longer where a contract or a market requires it.
  • One named contact for complaints, with a written response and a corrective action note.
  • We cooperate fully with port authorities, laboratories and regulators on any rapid alert or rejection.

Claims and corrections

Where we deliberately hold back

A buyer cannot verify a slogan, so we do not use them. Two commitments matter more than any adjective: we will not describe a consignment with a certificate we cannot produce, and we will tell you quickly when something goes wrong.

  • No organic claim without a valid certificate for the specific scope and lot.
  • No halal, kosher or GI claim without a certificate or authorised-user right that covers the sale.
  • No ISO, HACCP, FSSC 22000 or similar food safety certification claim for a site we do not operate or that does not hold it.
  • No "pesticide free", "chemical free", "100% pure" or "zero residue" language: residue levels are a laboratory measurement against a destination limit, not a marketing phrase.
  • No claim that a consignment will clear customs or pass a border test: those decisions belong to the importing authority.
  • No invented history, turnover, acreage, farmer counts or country lists.

If a consignment goes wrong

Send the lot code, the photographs and the test report to the contact address published on this site. We will trace the lot, respond in writing, and where the specification was not met we will correct it through replacement, credit or another remedy set out in the contract. We will also tell you what we changed so that it does not repeat, and we will not ask you to take down a truthful report while we investigate.

Want the documents for a specific lane?

Tell us the product and the destination port. We will send the specification sheet and the document list we propose to issue, so you can compare it with what your customs broker and your customer need.
