An exporter you can audit

We buy, process and ship Indian agricultural goods for importers, processors and retail brands. What we sell is not a mystery: it is a specification, a lot code and a document set. This page explains how the business works and who to speak to.

What we do

Sourcing, processing and export of four Indian ranges

We buy where the crop is grown

Spices from the belts that suit each one, makhana from the makhana belt of north Bihar, basmati from the notified basmati area, non-basmati rice from the southern and eastern rice states, and fresh produce close to the packhouse that handles it. Buying is against a grade, not against a photograph.

We process and grade to a written specification

Cleaning, sortex, milling, sizing, hand picking and packing are carried out to the sheet agreed with you. Where a step happens at a partner facility, we say so, and the same specification applies there.

We test the lot, not the story

Samples are drawn from the packed lot and analysed by NABL-accredited laboratories against the parameters your market tests for. The report travels with the consignment.

We treat paperwork as part of the job

Phytosanitary, origin, registration and destination-specific documents are prepared and checked before the container is sealed, not scrambled together at the port.

How we work

Six steps, in this order

The order matters. Agreeing a specification after the goods are packed, or testing after the container is sealed, is how disputes start. We would rather spend the time up front.

  1. 1. Enquiry and specification. You tell us the product, grade, quantity, packing, Incoterm and destination. We ask the awkward questions early: target moisture, the residue limit your market applies, the declarations the pack must carry.
  2. 2. Written offer. Availability, specification, packing, price basis and shipment window in writing, with the validity of the offer stated.
  3. 3. Sample and pre-shipment test. A lot sample is couriered with the laboratory parameters, so your technical team can compare the numbers with your own limits before you commit.
  4. 4. Processing and packing. Cleaning, grading, milling, hand picking and packing to the agreed net weight and marking, with a lot code assigned to the run.
  5. 5. Documents. The document set for your destination is prepared and checked, and the certificate wording is confirmed against what your broker and your customer need.
  6. 6. Shipment and follow-up. Loading under supervision, documents released against the agreed payment terms, and a deliberate follow-up after arrival so we learn what the market thought of the lot.
