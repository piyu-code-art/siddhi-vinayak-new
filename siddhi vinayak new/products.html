On cultivar names and geographical indications

Names such as Guntur Sannam, Byadagi or Alleppey Green are protected geographical indications. We use a GI name only where the goods come from the notified area and a valid authorised-user certificate covers the sale; otherwise the cultivar and the origin are stated plainly on the offer, the invoice and the packing.

Makhana (foxnut)

Makhana is graded on the suta scale, a traditional trade size notation. Larger grades suit retail shelf appeal; the smaller grades are just as good for grinding and coated products. Because suppliers do not all use identical sieves, we fix the size in millimetres on your specification sheet and confirm it against a pre-shipment sample.

Forty-five rice varieties are notified as basmati under the Seeds Act, 1966. Designations are quoted from published records and the variety actually shipped is confirmed on the invoice, the packing and the lot test report. Availability follows the season's crop.

Why the variety line matters

Only notified basmati varieties grown in the notified basmati area may be exported as basmati. Where the destination or the buyer requires it, we arrange variety authentication through the prescribed APEDA channel before shipment, and the export registration documents are obtained against the actual consignment. Sona Masuri, Samba Mahsuri and similar medium or long grain non-basmati lines are never described by us as basmati, in any document or on any pack. See the document checklist

Fresh produce moves under rules that change

Export of onion and other fresh lines is subject to the Directorate General of Foreign Trade policy in force at the time of shipment, which can include minimum export prices or temporary restrictions. We confirm the position for your proposed shipment date before accepting an order, and we will tell you if a policy prevents it. Availability, temperature set points and shelf-life expectations are always confirmed in writing for the specific lot.
